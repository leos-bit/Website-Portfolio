Solar-Powered Terrain Walker

Designed an autonomous, solar-powered four-legged robot that prioritized kinematic efficiency, lower cost, and reduced actuator count without sacrificing terrain performance.

Overview

Traditional legged robots often trade simplicity for capability. This project focused on a lighter, cheaper architecture that could still traverse terrain effectively.

Implementation

I used Chebyshev 4-bar linkages and pantographs to reduce each leg from three actuators to one. I modeled pivot designs in SolidWorks, validated structural integrity through ANSYS stress simulations, and developed motion and sensing subsystems in C on Arduino hardware.

Results

The design reduced overall cost by roughly 34%, simplified controls, and achieved terrain performance comparable to more complex six-legged systems.

My Contributions

  • Engineered the mechanism architecture and actuator reduction strategy.
  • Modeled and validated key structural components in CAD and FEA tools.
  • Implemented embedded sensing and gait control for stable locomotion.
