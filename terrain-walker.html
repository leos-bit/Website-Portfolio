Solar-Powered Terrain Walker

A low-cost autonomous quadruped that used linkage-based leg design to reduce actuation complexity without giving up terrain capability.

Focus Mechanism design and embedded controls
Tools SolidWorks, ANSYS, Arduino
Design Gain 34% lower cost than a comparable six-legged design
Actuation Reduced from three motors per leg to one

Overview

Many legged robots become expensive and fragile because each leg needs several actuators and a correspondingly complicated control system. This project explored a more deployable alternative: using clever linkage design to simplify locomotion mechanically before asking software to compensate.

Implementation

I designed the robot around Chebyshev 4-bar linkages and pantographs so each leg could be driven with a single actuator instead of three. I modeled the pivots and structural components in SolidWorks, validated key parts with ANSYS stress analysis, and implemented motion and sensing subsystems in C on Arduino hardware.

Results

The final design lowered cost by about 34%, simplified controls, and preserved useful terrain performance relative to more complex legged platforms. Consolidated actuation also improved energy efficiency and made the robot easier to build, debug, and reason about.

What I Built

  • Mechanism architecture based on Chebyshev 4-bar linkages and pantographs.
  • Actuator reduction strategy that cut each leg from three motors to one.
  • Structural design and validation in SolidWorks and ANSYS.
  • Embedded motion and sensing subsystems in C on Arduino.

Project Snapshot

The main idea was to push complexity into the mechanical design so the robot could move with fewer motors, simpler controls, and better energy efficiency.
