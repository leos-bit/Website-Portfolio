Solar-Powered Terrain Walker

Designed an autonomous, solar-powered four-legged robot that prioritized kinematic efficiency, lower cost, and reduced actuator count without sacrificing terrain performance.

Overview

Traditional legged robots often require multiple actuators per leg and a correspondingly complex control stack. This project focused on a lighter, cheaper architecture that could still traverse uneven terrain effectively while using fewer active components.

Implementation

I used Chebyshev 4-bar linkages and pantographs to reduce each leg from three actuators to one. I modeled pivot designs in SolidWorks, validated structural integrity through ANSYS stress simulations, and developed motion and sensing subsystems in C on Arduino hardware. The system combined mechanical simplification with embedded control logic to keep the gait stable while preserving the advantages of a legged platform.

Results

The design reduced overall cost by roughly 34%, simplified controls, and achieved terrain performance comparable to more complex six-legged systems. By consolidating actuation, the robot also improved energy efficiency and moved toward a more deployable low-cost locomotion platform.
